Background

Why Are 3pm Premier League Games Blacked Out?

The UK Broadcasting Blackout Rule (Section 166)

The 3pm blackout is a long-standing UK broadcasting law introduced in the 1960s under Section 166 of the Broadcasting Act 1996 (originally part of the 1960s protection rules). The rule states that no live football match can be broadcast on UK television between 2:45pm and 5:15pm on Saturdays.

Why does it exist? The Football Association and UK government introduced the blackout to protect attendances at lower-league football matches. The concern was that if every Premier League 3pm game was shown live on TV, fans would stay home instead of attending Championship, League One, League Two, and non-league matches that kick off at 3pm on Saturdays.

Who is affected? The blackout only applies to broadcasters based in the United Kingdom. Sky Sports, TNT Sports (formerly BT Sport), and BBC cannot show 3pm Saturday matches. However, international broadcasters — beIN Sports (Qatar/Middle East), SuperSport (South Africa), Canal+ (France), Viaplay (Scandinavia), Optus Sport (Australia) — have their own separate broadcasting rights and are not subject to UK law.

Key Takeaway

The 3pm blackout is a UK-specific regulation. International IPTV feeds from countries like Qatar (beIN Sports), South Africa (SuperSport), or France (Canal+) are perfectly legal to broadcast in their own territories — and there is no restriction on you accessing those international feeds via an IPTV subscription. This is the only reliable way to watch every 3pm Premier League match live outside of attending the stadium in person.
